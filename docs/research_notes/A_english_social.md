# A. English-language social media, forums and researcher blogs: evidence for "AI slop" signals in ML/AI papers (2025-2026)

Compiled 2026-10-05. Scope: X/Twitter, Reddit, Hacker News, OpenReview-adjacent venue blogs, researcher blogs/Substack/Medium, news coverage of those discussions.

## How to read this file

- **Verification legend**
  - `[V]`: page fetched during this research, and the quote/number appears in the fetched content. WebFetch returns model-extracted text, so quotes are as close to verbatim as the tool allowed. Re-check exact wording before publishing.
  - `[V-HN]`: Hacker News comment pulled directly from the HN Algolia API (`hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/<story>`). The quote is exact text and the comment id is given.
  - `[S]`: seen only as a search-engine result title/snippet. The page itself could not be fetched; X/Twitter blocks fetching via robots.txt. The tweet text shown is the indexed title.
  - `[U]`: unverified or low-reliability source. Use with caution.
- **Role** = whether the author identifies as a reviewer, AC, PC, editor or professor.
- **Access limits:** Reddit (www, old, JSON API, Arctic Shift, PullPush) was blocked or rate-limited from this environment. Bluesky's public API reset the connection. X pages are robots-blocked. Reddit content therefore appears only second-hand: a Reddit-linked HN story, and news reports that cite r/MachineLearning. Also unreachable: The Verge piece "AI research papers are getting better, and it's a big problem for scientists" (2026-05-15; site blocked), and statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2025/07/07/chatbot-prompts/ (HTTP 429). Both are noted but not quoted.

## 7. Notes on the specific complaints named in the brief

- **Heavy em-dash use:** well attested as a social-media tell (L1, L5, L15). Contested (C1, C2). Venue policies never use punctuation as evidence; they rely on detectors plus hallucinations.
- **Defensive writing:** no direct English-language source used this exact phrase about ML papers. Closest are "hedging accumulation" (L15) and ProofGap/CoherencePolish in BadScientist (R9). Treat as plausible but weakly sourced in English social media.
- **Coined terminology no one uses:** partial support from "hallucinated technical jargon" (geospatialml), "terminology that's technically correct but practically meaningless" (L9), and theoryslop's "proposes... new construct" (R1). The ICLR 2027 "paper-shaped objects" framing (R4) also fits.
- **Performative "honest disclosure" of failed experiments:** found no direct social-media quote. Mike Cook's "easier to get an AI to write about a failure convincingly" (R7) is the closest. A related research claim (Google paper reporting LLMs conceal negative results unless prompted for honesty; KuCoin news summary, not verified) suggests honesty-prompted agents may over-produce such sections.
- **Pivot to "auditing" a niche detail after the method fails:** no direct English quote found. COLM's "slopterpretability: carves off a narrow question... with off-the-shelf tools" (R2) is the closest venue-level description, along with "thin papers of narrow scope" from arXiv moderators (M13).
- **ARIS (Auto-Research-In-Sleep):** exists as an open-source skill pack (https://github.com/wanshuiyin/auto-claude-code-research-in-sleep) and an arXiv paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03042). No English social post was found that attributes specific conference submissions to ARIS by name.
